What the structure shows

Observations from the hierarchy itself. These are structural findings only — no policy assignments, RBAC, or resource contents were read.

Review

dt-sandbox sits on the tenant root, not in the Sandbox group

It is the only subscription attached directly to the root management group, so it inherits nothing from the Mercyhealth branch — none of the organisation-level policy, and none of the sandbox guardrails. Meanwhile the purpose-built Sandbox group immediately above it is empty.

New subscriptions land at the root by default, which is the usual way this happens. Moving it under Sandbox is a metadata move — it does not touch resources — but it does change which policies apply, so it wants a what-if review of the Sandbox group's assignments before anyone moves it.

Review

The Identity platform group has no subscription

Connectivity and Management both hold their platform subscriptions. Identity holds none, so there is no dedicated identity landing zone in this tenant — domain controllers or Entra Domain Services, if they exist, live somewhere else in the tree.

That is a legitimate design when identity is fully SaaS, but the empty group implies an intent that was not completed. Worth confirming which it is.

Note

Three lifecycle groups are staged but unused

Block Subscriptions, Decommissioned and Quarantined are all empty. This is the healthy state — they are destinations for subscriptions that need to be caught, retired, or isolated, and an empty Quarantined group means nothing is currently quarantined.

Their value depends entirely on the policy assigned to them, which this canvas does not cover.

Note

dt-prd-deployments-001 breaks both the naming and the placement pattern

Every other subscription carries the -oeqrq2jxadm36 tenant suffix; this one uses -001. It also hangs directly off Application Development rather than sitting in a workload group beside Connect, Microsites and TPA.

Both point to it being provisioned outside the normal vending process. Harmless in itself — but it means the naming standard is not being enforced by policy.

Note

Depth is at the edge of the recommended range

The deepest path — Root → Mercyhealth → Landing Zones → Application Development → Connect Application — is four levels below the root, against a hard ceiling of six. Cloud Adoption Framework guidance recommends keeping the tree to three or four levels.

There is room for two more, but adding another workload tier under Application Development would put the tree past what CAF advises and make inherited policy harder to reason about.

Note

Workload grouping is inconsistent across landing zones

Application Development nests each workload in its own group, so Connect, Microsites and TPA can each carry distinct policy. Data and Analytics puts its three subscriptions flat in one group, and AI Governance and Workday hold a single subscription each.

Flat is the right call at this size. It becomes a constraint only when two workloads in the same group need to diverge on policy.

Scope and evidence. Read-only. Everything above comes from one call to the ARM managementGroups/descendants endpoint plus az account list, both as hdesai@mhemail.org at 15:30 UTC on 3 September 2026. All 22 subscriptions visible to the CLI are accounted for in the tree, so nothing is missing at this permission level — though management groups this account cannot read would not appear.

Not covered. Policy assignments, initiatives and exemptions; RBAC and PIM; resource contents; cost. The findings are structural and none of them is a recommendation to change anything yet.
