Traffic flow

Routing Intent replaces the usual per-connection route tables with two policy routes, both next-hopping the firewall. The consequence is the shape below: spokes never talk to each other directly — east-west traffic hairpins up into the hub, through the firewall, and back down.

What the fabric shows

Structural and configuration findings from the live control plane and 30 days of gateway metrics. Nothing here has been changed.

Review

The two VPN tunnels terminate on the same on-premises IP

Site Azure-Mercyhealth and site MHTC-HA both point at 198.190.160.161. A second connection to the same physical endpoint adds no path diversity — if that device, its circuit, or its address becomes unreachable, both tunnels fail together. The name says HA; the configuration does not deliver it.

Real redundancy needs a second endpoint address, ideally on separate hardware and a separate circuit. This is the single largest availability gap in the hybrid path.

Unknown

The Mercyhealth tunnel has never reported a byte in 30 days

Azure Monitor returns exactly one tunnel dimension for this gateway — JVL-Egress. The Mercyhealth link on Connection-Azure-Mercyhealth produces no timeseries at all, and its ARM object returns a persistent GatewayError on every read attempt.

That combination is consistent with a tunnel that has never established, but I could not read its state directly to confirm it — so this is genuinely unknown rather than confirmed down. The portal's VPN connection blade, or an Entra-side gateway diagnostic, would settle it.

Review

ARM reports the live tunnel's status three different ways

Within minutes: the gateway object said NotConnected with zero counters; the connection object said Connected with 232 MB cumulative; Azure Monitor showed zero bytes for six hours but 68 MB across 16 active days in the last 30.

The metrics are the trustworthy view. Reading them together: JVL-Egress is a working tunnel carrying very little — a few MB on active days, silent for eleven straight days to 30 August, active 31 August through 2 September, and idle again today. Treat the control-plane status fields on this gateway as unreliable.

Review

The firewall is not zone-redundant, and everything depends on it

mercyhealth-azfw-northcentralus has no availability zones configured. Because Routing Intent sends east-west, north-south and hybrid traffic all through this one instance, its availability zone is the availability zone of the entire fabric — a zone failure in North Central US takes out spoke-to-spoke traffic as well as internet egress.

Adding zones to a hub firewall is a redeploy, not a setting, so this is a planned change rather than a quick fix. Worth sizing against the recovery objectives for the clinical workloads behind it.

Note

Standard tier means no IDPS and no TLS inspection

Both the firewall and its policy are Standard. Threat intelligence is set to Alert — it flags known-bad destinations but does not block them. IDPS and TLS inspection are Premium-only and are therefore absent.

For a healthcare estate this is worth a deliberate decision rather than a default. Moving threat intel to Alert and Deny is a policy toggle; Premium is a tier change with cost and latency implications.

Note

All egress leaves through a single public IP

Every spoke's internet traffic SNATs to 20.25.217.244. That is clean for allow-listing at partner firewalls, but it concentrates SNAT port capacity on one address and means one shared reputation for all sixteen spokes — dev traffic and production clinical traffic share an egress identity.

The firewall's own deny logs already show heavy volume to developer endpoints such as main.vscode-cdn.net, so dev egress is real and non-trivial.

Note

IPsec is negotiated without perfect forward secrecy

The JVL-Egress link uses IKEv2 with AES-256, SHA-256 and DH Group 14 for phase 1 — sound choices — but phase 2 sets PFS None with an 8-hour SA lifetime. Without PFS, compromise of the long-term key exposes previously captured traffic from that SA.

Enabling a PFS group requires a matching change on the Cisco side, so it is a coordinated change, not a one-sided toggle.

Note

Six subscriptions have no VNet at all

dt-shr-tpa, dt-workday, dt-prd-deployments-001, dt-prd-logging, dt-sandbox and the connectivity subscription itself hold no virtual network. For most that is expected — logging and deployments are control-plane workloads, and the hub VNet is Microsoft-managed.

dt-shr-tpa is the odd one: the other three TPA environments each have a spoke, and the shared one does not, even though Microsites has a shared spoke. Either the workload does not need one or it was never built.
